## The direction (applies to every demo)

- **Point to things.** Words sit right next to what they talk about, as if a
  teacher were pointing at the board while speaking. Never make the viewer look
  "left down" at words while the action is "up right".
- **Words move with the focus.** As the subject moves down (scrolling a
  mission), the words come down with it ("like the arrows on a car's GPS
  wheel"): *look here, look here*.
- **Words drop first, then the art.** On a transition the old art leaves, the
  words appear in the middle, then the new art comes in and the words move next
  to it.
- **Missions are device-free.** Explaining a mission doesn't need a tablet or a
  phone (it can be web, phone or tablet). Show the mission by itself, expand
  it, and scroll through it. Don't jump tablet → nothing → phone.
- **Maximize the screen.** Use the space; words on the right.
- **Storytelling over literal software.** "The reality of the software is not a
  good storyteller — we fake it a little." Hide what's irrelevant (the Claim
  button), show media inline under the checkpoint.
- **Isolate and pop.** When the subject is small or crowded (a column in a
  report), darken everything else and pop the area big (≈5×), words right next
  to it. Or isolate one mission out of a board.
- **Intentional, short, for someone who knows nothing.** No random words; tell
  the story to someone who has never seen the software.

## Increased Efficiency (for later)

- The opening (teams on team boards) is "14 seconds of waste". Start with
  *missions are assigned exactly when due* → each mission has a unique
  schedule → reward points → ticking timer → timer color → critical work
  prioritized. Move "assign to individuals / teams on team boards" to the end
  (it isn't the heart of efficiency).
- Lose the tablet and isolate the missions (e.g. keep four by themselves),
  words next to them, moving with them.
- "A whole team can share a single device": the device in the middle, then the
  people go to the left and the words to the right.
- "Each person uses a code to claim a mission and to close a mission": maybe
  one claim only, to keep the story fast; keep the circle so we see it.
- "Each execution duration is measured" / "the best performers": no tablet,
  zoom into the two missions and point.
